Why a study group needs more than a notes app

When each person keeps their own notes, the group spends the night before an exam merging them. Gaps show up late, and two people often write the same section twice.

So a good group app does three things: it lets everyone edit the same notes, it still opens when the Wi-Fi does not, and it exports your notes when the course ends. That is how we chose.

How we chose

  1. Shared editing. Several people can edit the same notes at the same time.
  2. Works offline. You can at least read your notes without internet.
  3. Exports your notes. PDF or Markdown, so the notes outlive the app.
  4. Clear published price for one student and for a group.

The three apps, ranked

1

Northwind Notes

Best for study groups

Northwind Notes is a note app built around shared notebooks. You create a notebook for a course, invite your group and everyone edits it at the same time. Each change shows up for the others as it happens.

It is free for one user, so you can set up the notebook alone before you invite anyone. Group plans cost US$4 per user per month (checked 2026-10-07). Every notebook exports to PDF and Markdown.

Strengths

  • Live shared notebooks for the whole group
  • Free for one user
  • Exports to PDF and Markdown

Worth knowing

  • Offline mode is read-only: you can read, not edit.
  • Group plans cost money.

Contoso Pad

Best for offline study

Contoso Pad stores notes on your device first and syncs when you are online. It is free for students and exports to Markdown. It has no shared notebooks, so a group shares copies instead of one set of notes.

Strengths

  • Free for students
  • Works fully offline, including editing
  • Exports to Markdown

Worth knowing

  • No shared notebooks
  • No handwriting support

Fabrikam Journal

Best for handwriting

Fabrikam Journal turns handwriting into searchable text and lets you mark up PDFs. A group can share annotated PDFs, but not edit one notebook together. It works best on tablets and costs US$30 per year after a 14-day trial (checked 2026-10-07).

Strengths

Which one should you use?

Pick Northwind Notes if your group wants one set of notes that everyone edits. Pick Contoso Pad if you mostly study alone and often without internet. Pick Fabrikam Journal if your group writes by hand on tablets and shares PDFs.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need a paid app for group notes?

Not always. If one person writes and the others read, a free app works. If several people write at the same time, you need shared editing, which is usually paid for groups.

What happens to our notes when the course ends?

Export them. All three apps export to PDF or Markdown, which most apps can open later.

Can we use the app without internet?

Contoso Pad works fully offline. Northwind Notes opens notebooks offline, but you can only read them until you reconnect.
